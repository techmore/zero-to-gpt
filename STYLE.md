# Writing Style Guide

Rules for anyone writing lessons, stories, timeline entries, or Bug Hunt challenges.

## Describing historical work: use their words

When we describe what a researcher did, we use **the words they used**, quoted and cited.

- Put the original wording in quotes and link the source:
  > Michie (1963) gave MENACE a box for each of "the 287 essentially distinct positions which the opening player can encounter" ([source](https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/6.3.232)). Most modern rebuilds use 304 boxes.
- If today's terms differ from the original, give the original first and the modern term after it:
  > McCulloch and Pitts called their units "neurons"; today we'd call them threshold logic units.
- Don't swap in our own paraphrase when it might change the meaning, the scope, or the date.
- If we can't find the original wording, say so ("we couldn't find the original source for this") instead of guessing.
- Open the source before you quote it. An earlier draft of this guide quoted Gray et al. (1995) on "the sex classification task" and linked a PDF that turned out to be a *different* paper (Fellous, 1997, *Vision Research*). Even style guides need a Bug Hunt.

**Key terms, in their words** (each one checked against the original paper):

| They said | Who, where | Today we say |
|---|---|---|
| "neurons" with "all-or-none" activity, treated "by means of propositional logic" | McCulloch & Pitts 1943 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02478259)) | threshold logic units |
| "perceptron": "a probabilistic model for information storage and organization in the brain" | Rosenblatt 1958 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042519)) | single-layer linear classifier |
| "Two machine-learning procedures have been investigated…" | Samuel 1959 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.33.0210)) | machine learning (one of the term's earliest uses) |
| "(soft-)search", "align", "alignment model"; "attention" appears only twice | Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio 2014 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)) | attention |
| "the 287 essentially distinct positions" | Michie 1963 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/6.3.232)) | 304 boxes in most modern MENACE rebuilds |
| "Shortcuts are decision rules that perform well on standard benchmarks but fail to transfer…" | Geirhos et al. 2020 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-020-00257-z)) | shortcut learning |

**Why:** paraphrasing is how history gets bent. A real result gets attached to the wrong machine, the wrong decade, or a bigger claim than the authors made. Quoting the source keeps us honest. It also shows students how to check claims, which is the point of the Bug Hunt.

## Sensitive topics

Studies that classify people (by sex, race, faces, and so on) are history we can discuss, quoting the authors' own terms. They are never hands-on student activities. We treat them as conversations about bias and fairness.

## Facts and links

- Every historical claim needs a source. If it's uncertain, mark it as uncertain or leave it out.
- Never make up a URL. Verify links before publishing.

## Reading level and values language

- Kid-facing text (Kid version, Bug Hunt challenges, classroom handouts) aims at a 6th-grade reading level: short sentences, everyday words, one idea at a time.
- The course grew up in a school with Friends-school heritage, a community that values contemplation, silence, intentionality and context. Keep values language light and optional ("Something to think about (optional): …"). Teachers can use their own school's words or skip it. Don't describe activities as religious practice, and don't name any school.
