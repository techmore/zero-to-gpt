### Meet "Buddy," our classroom AI helper

Part of the course uses an AI helper we call **Buddy**. Some things you should know:

- **Buddy runs locally, on a computer here on campus** ([e.g., the school's Mac mini]).
  It uses a small open language model, [model name], that runs on that computer. It is

- **No student data leaves campus.** What students type to Buddy is processed on that

- Many activities use a **scripted** Buddy: a program with pre-written answers and no

### Buddy makes mistakes on purpose (and students know it)

We call this **Bug Hunters**. Buddy is set up to sometimes give **wrong answers on
purpose**: a wrong count, a made-up "fact," or code with a hidden bug. Students are
told this **up front**, before they ever use Buddy. Their job is to catch the
mistakes by checking evidence and writing small tests. Sometimes Buddy is right, and
then students must *prove* it's right instead of assuming it's wrong.

Why do it this way? Real AI tools make mistakes, often in a confident voice. We want
students to practice the habit of **checking claims** before repeating them, which is
a matter of honesty as much as of computing. They also learn that a computer program "guessing the next word" is not
the same thing as knowing what's true.
